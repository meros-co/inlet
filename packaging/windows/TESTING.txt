Inlet test build for Windows (unsigned)
=======================================

What this is: an AES67 virtual sound card. Your DAW sees an ASIO device called
"Inlet"; its inputs are AES67 streams from the network and its outputs are sent
to the network as AES67 streams. A Manifold console (or any NMOS controller,
such as Sluice) can find and route it.

Needs: Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11, x64, a WIRED network interface on the
AES67 network (Wi-Fi cannot carry the streams), and an ASIO host (REAPER,
Cubase, Studio One, ...). A PTP grandmaster on the network for clock lock;
without one, Inlet runs on the computer's own clock and says so.

Install
-------
1. Unzip the folder anywhere.
2. Double-click install.cmd and accept the administrator prompt. (Windows
   SmartScreen may warn that the files are unsigned: "More info" -> "Run anyway".)
3. The Inlet app opens. Its tray icon (the Inlet mark) has a green dot when PTP
   is locked, amber when free-running, grey when Inlet is stopped.
   Installing over an earlier build is fine (settings are kept), and is needed
   once for the app's "Start Inlet" button to work: earlier builds did not let
   a signed-in user start the service.

Check
-----
- Inlet app, Status page: the network interface should be your wired AES67
  interface. If not, choose it on the Settings page and press Apply.
- Status page, Clock: "Locked to the grandmaster" when your network has one.
- In your DAW, choose the ASIO device "Inlet". The Status page lists the DAW
  under "Programs using Inlet". The DAW's ASIO control panel button opens the
  Inlet app.

Receive (network -> DAW)
------------------------
- From a console or Sluice: route a sender to an Inlet receiver ("Net In 1" ...).
  Net In k's channels are DAW inputs (k-1)*8+1 ... k*8 by default.
- Or in the Inlet app, Receivers page: pick a stream (heard by SAP or NMOS, or
  pasted with "Add an SDP..."), select a receiver, "Play on this receiver".

Send (DAW -> network)
---------------------
- DAW outputs 1-8 go out as "Net Out 1", 9-16 as "Net Out 2", and so on,
  announced by SAP and NMOS. Route them from the console or Sluice.
- Several programs may play out through Inlet at once (up to eight): their
  outputs are mixed. The Status page says which programs send their outputs.

DAW sample rates
----------------
- The DAW may run at 44.1, 48, 88.2, 96, 176.4 or 192 kHz, whatever the
  network's rate: Inlet converts. The Status page shows a program's own rate
  next to it when Inlet converts for it.
- Converting adds a little latency (a few milliseconds at most), which Inlet
  reports to the DAW, so its delay compensation stays exact. At the
  network's rate nothing is converted.

Start, Stop and quitting
------------------------
- Status page, "Stop Inlet" (top right), or the tray menu's "Stop Inlet": the
  service (inletd) quits. Inlet's senders leave the network (SAP deletions,
  NMOS deregistration and an mDNS goodbye) and its receivers stop. A DAW
  using Inlet keeps running and hears silence. If a DAW is using Inlet, the
  app asks first.
- Status page, "Start Inlet" (shown while stopped), or the tray menu's "Start
  Inlet": the service starts and the app reconnects within a few seconds. A
  DAW that kept Inlet open carries on by itself (within half a second of the
  service starting); nothing needs reopening.
- Changing the channel counts or the sample rate on the Settings page asks a
  DAW using Inlet to reset (it reopens the device itself); other settings
  don't interrupt it.
- Tray menu, "Quit the Inlet app": the window and tray icon go; the sound card
  keeps running. Start "Inlet" from the Start menu to bring the app back.
- Tray menu, "Quit Inlet completely": stops the service as above, then quits
  the app. Inlet comes back when you open the Inlet app and press "Start
  Inlet", at your next sign-in (the app starts in the tray and starts the
  service), or when Windows restarts (the service starts with Windows).
- Closing the window only hides it; the tray icon stays.

Loopback self-test (no other device needed)
-------------------------------------------
1. Close your DAW (Inlet mixes every program's outputs, and the DAW's would
   be added to the test's).
2. Inlet app, Receivers page: select "Net In 1", choose the stream "Net Out 1",
   "Play on this receiver".
3. In a command prompt in the installed folder (C:\Program Files\Inlet\tools):
     inlet-asio-loopback.exe ..\inlet_asio.dll 15 128
   It should end with "PASS: every sample came back, at one fixed latency".
4. The same with the DAW-side rate converted, for example at 44.1 kHz:
     inlet-asio-loopback.exe ..\inlet_asio.dll 15 128 44100
   It should end with "PASS: the samples came back through the converters,
   at one round trip".

Where things are
----------------
- Program:   C:\Program Files\Inlet
- Settings:  C:\ProgramData\Inlet\inletd.json   (kept on uninstall)
- Log:       C:\ProgramData\Inlet\inletd.log
- Service:   "Inlet" (inletd) in services.msc
- API:       http://127.0.0.1:8091/inlet/v1/status (and NMOS at /x-nmos/)

Uninstall
---------
Double-click uninstall.cmd in C:\Program Files\Inlet (or in this folder).

Known limits of this build
--------------------------
- Up to eight programs play out at once; a ninth plays Inlet's inputs only.
- 32-sample ASIO buffers may click; 64 and above are recommended.
- macOS is not in this build.
